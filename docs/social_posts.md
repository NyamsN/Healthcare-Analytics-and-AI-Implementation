Week 3 at AnalystLab Africa done! 🏥📊

As a Data Science intern, I investigated what drives hospital encounter costs using 27,891 encounters, 974 patients and 47,701 procedure records (synthetic Synthea data).

What I found:
• Encounter class matters, but only moderately: it explains 7–15% of cost variation
• Procedures are the real cost driver: in every class, encounters with a procedure cost far more (up to 158× the median in urgent care)
• Raw comparisons mislead: once procedures are held constant, wellness visits lose most of their cost premium, while emergency and urgent care flip from "cheap" to "expensive"
• Zero payer coverage is about the payer, not the type of visit

The biggest lesson: ten patients produced a fifth of all encounters, so every test had to be re-checked at patient level. And I never loaded a single patient name or address.

Tools: Python, pandas, SciPy, statsmodels, Seaborn, SQL.
